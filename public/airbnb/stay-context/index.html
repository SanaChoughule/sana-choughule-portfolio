THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Not just “Where is this stay?” but “How does this stay fit my trip?”

Stay Context starts from a simple observation: a property’s location is meaningful only when a traveler can relate it to their arrival point, plans, priorities and constraints.

01 · THE CHALLENGE

A good listing doesn’t always answer the location question.

Travelers compare price, photos, amenities and reviews—but still need to work out what a property’s location means for their own plans.

To do that, they may move between accommodation listings, search, maps, social content and conversations with people they trust. Each source offers a piece of the picture; the traveler has to connect those pieces themselves.

01

How far is the stay from the place I arrive?

02

How does it relate to the places I want to visit?

03

What travel time should I realistically allow?

04

Am I making a choice that fits my trip—not just the listing?

THE OPPORTUNITY

Reduce the effort of connecting a property to a traveler’s plans, and bring useful context closer to the booking decision.

02 · DISCOVERY & APPROACH

Start with the journey, not the feature.

I explored the problem through a combination of personal travel observations, a small survey, informal conversations with friends and family, and competitive/product research.

The inputs were directional and qualitative. I used them to look for recurring planning friction—not to claim a statistically representative view of all travelers.

WHAT STOOD OUT

Location is not just a pin. It’s a relationship between the stay and the trip.

The same property can feel like the right choice for one itinerary and the wrong fit for another. The traveler needs context to make that judgement for themselves.

PRODUCT OPPORTUNITY

Help travelers understand the practical implications of a property’s location before booking, without making them manually research across multiple platforms.

03 · THE PRODUCT CONCEPT

Bring trip context into the stay decision.

Stay Context is imagined as an additive experience within a property page. It builds on accommodation discovery rather than trying to become another maps or itinerary product.

04 · PRODUCT JUDGEMENT

Useful context. Not another verdict.

PRINCIPLE 01

Inform, don’t judge

Show factual context rather than assigning a location score, ranking properties or declaring a winner. What works depends on the traveler.

PRINCIPLE 02

Respect location privacy

Before booking, provide decision-useful approximate context without exposing a host’s exact private address.

PRINCIPLE 03

Extend, don’t replace

Keep existing listing, review, booking and reservation experiences intact. Add context where it helps the decision.

PRINCIPLE 04

Keep the traveler in control

The traveler chooses the arrival point and places that matter. The product supports the decision; it doesn’t plan the trip for them.

ONE DELIBERATE SCOPE CHOICE

This is not a new navigation system or full itinerary planner. The first focus is a narrower question: understanding a stay’s location in relation to a trip before booking.

WHAT THE PROTOTYPE DOES — AND DOESN’T — PROVE

The functional prototype helped make the interaction model tangible and supported an owner-led test cycle. It is a concept demonstration: it does not represent a live Airbnb integration, production routing accuracy, externally moderated usability validation or measured business impact.

06 · WHAT I TOOK AWAY

Design for the decision the person is actually making.

This project reinforced that useful product work often begins by reframing a familiar problem. A map can show where a stay is; the bigger opportunity is helping someone understand what that location means for their plans.

It also made the trade-offs tangible: provide enough information to support confidence, protect location privacy, keep the experience focused and make the concept concrete enough to discuss with design and engineering.
