THE PRODUCT THOUGHT

Make it easier to decide where to go—and make room for it in the day.

PlanIt brings two connected planning needs into one product direction: turning an outing idea into an editable itinerary, and organising everyday commitments through schedules, priorities, focus and habits.

01 · DISCOVERY & PROBLEM FRAMING

Planning takes effort before the plan even begins.

Planning a date or outing can mean switching between maps, restaurant discovery, movie listings, activity options, weather and booking platforms before everyone settles on a plan.

The person organising the outing has to connect the options, fit them into available time and budget, and turn a scattered set of ideas into a practical sequence. A list of places is not always the same as a plan that works together.

The everyday side has a related planning challenge: tasks, time blocks, priorities, focus sessions and personal routines can sit in separate places, making it harder to see how the day is actually being used.

PROBLEM FRAMING

Two connected questions:

01

How might someone move from an outing idea to a practical, editable itinerary with less switching between services?

02

How might someone organise tasks, focus and routines so they can make time for the things they plan?

PRODUCT OPPORTUNITY

Bring context, choices and next steps into one planning experience—without taking away the user's ability to change the plan or choose the services they use.

This section reflects the problem framing and product opportunity documented in the main portfolio case study. It does not claim a quantified research sample or measured user-study results.

PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

The plan should be a useful starting point—not a fixed recommendation. People can edit, replace and reorder what does not work for them.

03 · OUTING PLANNING EXPERIENCE

From scattered options to one connected itinerary.

01Set preferences

Choose interests, frequently visited Mumbai areas, outing frequency and indoor, outdoor or mixed preferences.

02Set the context

Use GPS with permission or enter a location manually. Add start time, duration, budget, mood, company and travel-distance preference.

03Generate a route

Connect a cafe or restaurant, an activity such as a movie or gaming, and a final stop such as dessert, a walk, gallery or seaside visit.

04Refine the plan

Replace a suggestion, reorder stops or adjust the inputs without restarting the whole planning journey.

05Hand off when ready

Open relevant third-party information, booking or directions services. The partner handles the transaction.

04 · DAILY PLANNING EXPERIENCE

Make the time visible. Make the next step clearer.

The second prototype explores how everyday execution can sit alongside outing planning as part of the same unified product concept.

01 / SCHEDULE

Seven-day timeline

Capture tasks, move across dates, view daily and weekly schedules, mark blocks complete and place unscheduled tasks into open time.

02 / PRIORITISE

Eisenhower matrix

Review work by importance and urgency, with Kanban and list views to support different ways of organising tasks.

03 / FOCUS

Focus and rest cycles

Use timed focus and rest sessions to create protected periods for concentrated work.

04 / BUILD ROUTINES

Habits and streaks

Check in on daily rituals and see consistency over time through habit tracking.

05 / REVIEW

Weekly insights

Explore task completion, focus time and habit consistency, with copyable summaries and JSON export.

06 / ADAPT

Auto-schedule gaps

Place eligible unscheduled tasks into available time while preserving existing blocks, then review the proposed schedule.

The analytics and progress views in the browser prototype are calculated from illustrative and browser-session data. They are not connected to an external calendar, account or analytics service.

05 · PRODUCT DECISIONS & BOUNDARIES

Useful assistance, without taking control away.

01

Personalise from context

Use selected interests, location, time, budget, mood, company and indoor/outdoor preferences to shape the plan.

02

Keep plans editable

Allow people to replace activities, reorder stops and change constraints without restarting.

03

Hand off transactions

Do not process payments or own partner bookings. Redirect to services such as Zomato, BookMyShow and Google Maps.

04

Minimise stored personal data

The product brief excludes a permanent user database. Preferences and favourites are intended for local, session or in-memory use and cleared when the session expires.

05

Offer location choice

Use device location only with permission, while keeping manual location entry available.

06

Keep the scope extensible

Start with defined activity categories and Mumbai context, with a product direction designed to extend to more cities and categories.

STATE & PRIVACY

Session-based or in-memory data is proposed. A permanent user database and persistent personal profiles are outside the stated brief.

EXTERNAL HAND-OFF

Booking and navigation links redirect to third-party providers; payment and booking transactions remain with those providers.

NON-FUNCTIONAL TARGET

The brief sets a plan-generation target of under three seconds. This is a requirement to validate, not a measured prototype result.

DEPENDENCIES TO VALIDATE

Place discovery, route estimates, directions, opening hours, weather, provider coverage, rate limits, accuracy and graceful failure behaviour.

IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

The native frontend choice between React Native and Flutter, exact data providers, API coverage and failure-handling approach remain to be confirmed. The two browser previews use illustrative/sample data and do not demonstrate a connected Java backend or live provider integrations.

08 · WHAT THIS PROJECT BRINGS TOGETHER

Product thinking with a builder’s perspective.

PlanIt brings together discovery and problem framing, product definition, editable user experiences, explicit privacy boundaries and a proposed technical direction.

It also creates a useful bridge between the customer-facing question—“What should I do with my time?”—and the system questions behind it: where context comes from, how a plan is assembled, what stays in session, which providers own a transaction and how the experience responds when data is unavailable.
