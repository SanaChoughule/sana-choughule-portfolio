01 / THE CHALLENGE

The portfolio isn't the hard part.
Keeping it current is.

Sharing a new project on a social platform can be quick. Updating a professional website is a separate sequence of small jobs—and that sequence repeats every time fresh work is ready.

01

Find & prepare

Locate the original media, check the right file, create a thumbnail and make sure the asset is ready for the website.

02

Describe & organise

Write a title and description, add tags, choose a category and decide how the project should sit within the existing grid.

03

Publish & verify

Check the desktop and mobile presentation, publish the update and confirm the portfolio reflects the intended work.

✳
THE PRODUCT INSIGHT

The gap is between producing creative work and maintaining the professional portfolio that represents it.

PortfolioPilot is designed around that hand-off—not around automating social posts or replacing the creator’s editorial judgement.

02 / STAKEHOLDER CONTEXT

One workflow.
Three perspectives.

The concept starts with the independent creator who manages both the creative work and the website. It also considers the people who help manage portfolios and the clients who rely on them to assess work.

This is a product framing of stakeholder needs and assumptions to validate—not a claim of representative user research or a measured study.

PRIMARY STAKEHOLDER

Independent creators

Photographers and videographers who need their professional presence to stay current without losing time to repetitive website administration.

SECONDARY STAKEHOLDER

Creative teams & agencies

People coordinating more than one body of work or helping maintain multiple professional portfolios.

END VISITOR

Prospective clients

People who need a clear, current and easy-to-navigate view of a creator’s work before starting a conversation.

04 / KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS

Useful automation.
Uncompromised authorship.

The product should remove repetitive setup work without making creative choices on the user’s behalf or unexpectedly changing the portfolio’s visual identity.

DECISION 01

Direct upload is the reliable default

Do not make the core workflow depend on social-platform access. Treat an Instagram URL as a convenience when retrieval is explicitly supported and authorised.

RELIABILITY BEFORE CONVENIENCE
DECISION 02

AI recommends. The creator edits.

Titles, descriptions, tags and categories are starting suggestions—not final creative copy or silently published decisions.

ASSIST, DON'T OVERRIDE
DECISION 03

Use the creator’s own taxonomy

Categories should reflect the creator’s discipline and way of presenting work, rather than forcing a fixed universal set.

FLEXIBLE CLASSIFICATION
DECISION 04

Separate content from presentation

Adding a new portfolio item should not unexpectedly redesign the website, reorder everything or change an established visual identity.

PRESERVE THE EXISTING BRAND
DECISION 05

Keep approval in the publishing path

Creators can review, edit, publish or save as draft. Automatic publishing is not the default for the first release.

HUMAN REVIEW BEFORE PUBLISH
05 / MVP FOCUS & PRIORITISATION

Start with the smallest
complete publishing loop.

The first release is valuable only if a creator can take one new asset all the way through to a controlled portfolio update. Advanced management can follow after that workflow is validated.

MVP / CORE WORKFLOW

Everything needed to finish one update.

  • 01Create a portfolio workspace and configure creator-defined categories.
  • 02Upload an image or video and validate the file.
  • 03Prepare a thumbnail or representative frame and draft metadata suggestions.
  • 04Review, edit, approve or save the portfolio item as a draft.
  • 05Publish approved work and manage existing portfolio items.
LATER / AFTER CORE VALIDATION

Expand only when the basics work.

  • Multi-user agency workspaces and role-based collaboration.
  • Managing multiple creator portfolios from one account.
  • Custom domains and deeper website integrations.
  • Portfolio analytics and richer performance insights.
  • Broader social publishing or cross-platform distribution.

These are deliberately outside the first release so the core creator workflow can be tested without adding unnecessary surface area.

PRODUCT QUESTIONS

What is the minimum information a creator must provide? Which suggestions save time? When must a person approve? What happens when the workflow cannot complete?

ENGINEERING QUESTIONS

Where are source files stored? How are large media files processed? Which website stacks can be supported? How do we handle retries, duplicate publishing and access permissions?

07 / SUCCESS SIGNALS & GUARDRAILS

Measure the effort removed.
Protect the control retained.

The first validation should show whether the workflow removes administrative work while keeping the quality and ownership of the public portfolio intact.

Upload-to-publish timeHow long does one complete portfolio update take?
Manual steps per itemWhich repeated actions are actually removed?
Suggestion correction rateHow often do creators need to rewrite metadata or change categories?
Processing success & recoveryCan the workflow handle unsupported or incomplete media clearly?
Portfolio freshnessHow long does approved work take to appear on the public site?
Publishing guardrailsAre duplicate, accidental or unapproved publishes prevented?

These are proposed measures to validate and baselines to establish—not achieved results or proven business impact.

i
Prototype boundary — please read

This is a browser-based concept demo using sample content and local interactions. The processing timeline and metadata suggestions are simulated placeholders: there is no live Gemini call, background worker, cloud media upload, Instagram media retrieval or remote website publishing. Instagram URL entry checks its format only. Uploaded assets remain temporary in the browser session, while workspace edits are stored locally in the browser. The demo is not a production-connected product.
